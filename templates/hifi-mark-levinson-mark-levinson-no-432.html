About the Mark Levinson No. 432 & Our Approach

The Mark Levinson No. 432 stereo amplifier (circa 2000-2007) is a reference-grade power amplifier from the legendary 43x series. Delivering 400 watts per channel into 8 ohms (800 watts into 4 ohms), the No. 432 features a fully balanced, dual-mono design, massive toroidal transformers, and high-bias Class A/AB operation. Deep Dive: Our Mark Levinson No. 432 Restoration Process

⚡ POWER SUPPLY (Massive & Critical)
  • Main Filter Capacitors: Massive snap-in or screw-terminal electrolytics (typically 8-12 large caps total, 10,000-22,000µF each) – we replace with high-ripple, low-ESR, 105°C rated types (Nichicon KG, Cornell Dubilier, or Kemet).
  • Bridge Rectifiers: We test and replace if noisy, upgrade to ultra-fast soft recovery types for lower noise floor and cleaner power delivery.
  • Soft Start / Thermistors: We check inrush current limiters and replace if cracked or out of spec.
  • Secondary Filter Caps: All smaller electrolytics on the power supply board and driver boards are replaced.
  • Transformer Check: We verify massive toroidal transformer output voltages and listen for mechanical hum.
🎛️ DRIVER BOARD RESTORATION (Dual-Mono Design)
COMPLETE SIGNAL PATH RESTORATION:
  • Electrolytic Capacitors: All coupling and power supply decoupling caps on the driver boards are replaced with audio-grade types (Nichicon FG, Elna Silmic II, or WIMA films for small values).
  • Small Signal Transistors: We test differential pair transistors and replace if noisy or mismatched. Matched pairs ensure lowest DC offset.
  • Feedback Network: We verify resistor values and replace any that have drifted.
  • Gain Stage Service: We check all active components, verify proper voltage biasing.
🔊 OUTPUT STAGE & THERMAL MANAGEMENT
  • Output Transistor Testing: The No. 432 uses multiple pairs of high-power transistors per channel (typically 8-12 pairs per channel, TO-3P or TO-247 packages). We test each transistor for gain (hFE), leakage, and breakdown voltage. We replace failed or mismatched devices with genuine, matched replacements.
  • Emitter Resistors: We measure and replace any out-of-tolerance emitter resistors (typically 0.22Ω-0.47Ω, 5W).
  • Thermal Compound Replacement: We remove old, dried thermal compound from output transistors and massive heatsinks, apply fresh high-quality thermal paste for optimal heat transfer.
  • Bias Transistor (Vbe Multiplier): We check the bias spreader transistor and ensure proper thermal tracking.
⚙️ BIAS CALIBRATION & THERMAL STABILITY
  • Initial Bias Setting: Using a precision DMM, we set bias current per channel to factory specifications (typically 75-150mA depending on version).
  • Warm-Up Stabilization: We allow the amplifier to reach thermal equilibrium (45-60 minutes) and re-check bias.
  • Thermal Tracking Verification: We monitor bias current as the amplifier heats up to ensure stable operation.
  • Final Bias Adjustment: We lock in bias settings and verify distortion at low and high power levels.
🛡️ PROTECTION CIRCUIT
  • Speaker Relay: We test the output relay for contact resistance, replace if pitted or intermittent.
  • DC Offset Protection: We verify the DC detection circuit triggers correctly.
  • Thermal Cutoff: We test thermal switches mounted on heatsinks.
  • Over-Current Sensing: We check current sense resistors and comparator circuits.

Common Mark Levinson No. 432 Problems we Solve

#1 Power Supply Capacitor Aging

Symptom: Increased noise floor, loss of dynamics, reduced bass authority, hum.
Our Solution: Complete recap of main filter capacitors and all secondary power supply electrolytics.

#2 Output Transistor Failure

Symptom: Amplifier goes into protection, distorted output, no sound from one channel, blown fuse, DC at output.
Our Solution: Test all output transistors, replace failed devices with genuine, matched replacements, recalibrate bias.

#3 DC Offset Drift

Symptom: Protection relay engages intermittently, audible thump on power on/off, DC at speaker terminals.
Our Solution: Replace differential pair transistors, adjust DC offset to near 0mV, check servo circuit capacitors.

#4 Bias Drift / Unstable Bias

Symptom: Amplifier runs too hot, distortion at low levels, bias current fluctuates.
Our Solution: Replace bias trim pots, check Vbe multiplier transistor, recalibrate bias to factory specs.

#5 Speaker Relay Not Engaging

Symptom: No sound, relay clicks but no output, intermittent output.
Our Solution: Replace output relay, check protection circuit capacitors, verify DC offset.

#6 One Channel Distorted / Low Output

Symptom: Imbalance between channels, distortion on one side.
Our Solution: Check driver board components, test output transistors, verify power supply rails.

#7 Excessive Heat / Thermal Shutdown

Symptom: Amplifier shuts down after playing loudly, runs extremely hot.
Our Solution: Check bias current (likely too high), replace dried thermal compound, verify heatsink mounting, test thermal switches.
