Our Step-by-Step Repair Process

  1. 1 First Look & Basic Clean

    What we do: We power down and unplug your lens, check the outside for dings or cracks, and blow dust off the body with clean air. We don't open the lens for inside dust unless it's in our clean room.

    Tools & tips: Microfiber wipes, canned air, lens brushes. No harsh chemicals on the special coatings.

    Typical problems: Surface dust buildup, light condensation spots.

  2. 2 Outer Mechanics Test

    What we do: We spin the zoom and focus rings to feel for smoothness. If they're gummed up, we apply approved lubricant. We also inspect the mount for warps or bends.

    Tools & tips: Precision torque drivers and fluorocarbon-type grease, with screws held to 0.5–1.0 Nm.

    Typical problems: Stuck controls from dirt, sand or salt exposure.

  3. 3 Getting to the Glass

    What we do: If the picture is still soft, we remove the caps and front guard (usually 4–6 screws on box lenses), clean the elements and realign them on our collimator setup.

    Tools & tips: Lens-safe tissues, high-purity isopropyl (90%+) and collimator tools, all in a dust-controlled zone.

    Typical problems: Mold spots, fungus growth, water marks.

  4. 4 Impact Damage Assessment

    What we do: Dropping a lens may leave no visible damage to the glass or body, but the delicate internals can still be damaged. If your lens has taken a hit, you will notice a problem even without scratches, dents or scrapes.

    Signs: an unfamiliar sound, stiff or uneven movement, or distorted image quality. A thin knife or needle-nose pliers may move a bent ring, but they can easily damage delicate metal or circuits.

    Our advice: Bring it in for impact service.

  5. 5 Electronics & Servo Check

    What we do: We connect the lens to a controller such as the Fujinon ERD-10A and run diagnostics to find encoder glitches. Bad boards are replaced.

    Tools & tips: Digital multimeters, scopes and manufacturer diagnostic software.

    Typical problems: Slow servos, glitchy encoders from water damage.

  6. 6 Stabilizer Tune-Up

    What we do: We shake-test the image stabilizer on our rigs and recalibrate it for correct pitch and yaw balance.

    Tools & tips: Vibration tables and manufacturer calibration software.

    Typical problems: Shaky footage from bumps or wind, alignment shifts after drops.

  7. 7 Reassembly & Bench Test

    What we do: We reassemble everything in reverse order and tighten screws to spec. Then full tests: zoom through the range, iris checks, bokeh smoothness and 4K sharpness on our projectors.

    Tools & tips: Test projectors and 4K charts. We watch for focus breathing.

    Typical problems: General performance of the whole lens.

  8. 8 Seal It Up

    What we do: If the old coatings are worn, we add fresh anti-fog layers and seal the housings with silicone gaskets.

    Tools & tips: UV sealants for curing.

    Typical problems: Stopping future moisture leaks.
